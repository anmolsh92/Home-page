In-house studio or agency?

Demand for AI content is high, but that isn't the same as teams being ready to make it themselves. The pull between managed services and empowered in-house creative teams will shape how marketing-heavy brands work next.

There's been a huge demand for AI-generated content over the last year. But wanting AI content and being ready to make it yourself with content agents are two very different things. Most brands I've worked with sit somewhere in between. I think the tussle between a growing managed service model and the wish for strong in-house creative teams is what will set the industry's dynamics for the next few years.

Since mid 2025, I've worked closely with brand, marketing and content teams at many of the top companies in India. Most of them are in categories that spend heavily on marketing, like D2C, FMCG and fashion, plus a few others. These are some of the things I've learned from that GTM work.

1Demand keeps growing

The demand hasn't slowed down since last year. When I ask teams why they want AI content, the answer usually comes back as one of three things.

  • They don't want to fall behind. Peers are trying every new AI trend, and nobody wants to be the brand that missed it.

  • They're excited about making something that hasn't been done before.

  • They want big cost savings.

2Demand isn't readiness

Demand for AI-generated content shouldn't be read as readiness for AI agents, though. Adoption of agents still has a long way to go. The DIY mode hasn't picked up the way we expected it to, and most teams still prefer an agency to produce their AI content. From what I've seen, there are four main reasons.

  1. 1

    The first is plain inertia. Teams have worked a certain way for years, and changing that takes more than a good tool.

  2. 2

    The second is how much they lean on an external partner. A lot of brand teams brief, review and approve, but don't do much hands-on work themselves. Picking up an agent means building a muscle they haven't needed so far.

  3. 3

    Then there's structure. Many of these companies have fairly heavy hierarchies, and that keeps the appetite for risk low. If AI takes away the realism and the content falls flat, it's a lot easier when that risk sits with the agency.

  4. 4

    And finally, cost per unit. An agency is cheap enough per piece that you can ask for many retries, a round of changes and the odd last-minute emergency without thinking twice.

3Managed services get stronger

Even the big agentic AI content workflow companies from the West have started managed service verticals, with a focus on India and parts of Southeast Asia. That says a lot about where the demand really is right now.

Agencies are guarding their share of marketing spend too, and AI is helping them do it. Less prompting effort and tools that simplify repetitive workflows let them deliver faster, so they've been filling part of the demand for AI content themselves.

4The silver lining for in-house teams

Still, several top brand agencies and marketing publications have pointed to a silver lining. The scale is small for now, but companies clearly want strong in-house creative teams.

Part of it is culture. When creative discussions happen on the floor, among people who live with the brand every day, they build a sense of belonging and spark ideas that a briefing call rarely does.

Agility

You hear stories of a few young interns at an early-stage company beating a marketing giant. That kind of story only happens with an in-house team. An outsourced partner can't move with that speed.

5Spend is being recalibrated

Marketing budgets are being reshuffled as well. Brands want agencies on higher-value work like brand videos and recall content. Repurposing, ideation and shortening the creation window are moving in-house.

D2C has an interesting twist. As more affluent Indians travel abroad, they've come back with a liking for the in-store experiences that lifestyle and luxury brands offer. Brands will try to bring that here, even through promotional stores, and that's work where agencies will play a bigger role.

I think the push for empowered in-house creative teams will drive adoption of agentic content workflows. But inertia isn't going away soon, and that leaves plenty of room for the managed service model, where freelancers or small teams deliver AI content for brands. For a while at least, I expect the two to grow side by side.
